# 02 — Investor Pitch (Deck Script + Narrative)

> A complete pitch you can deliver in 3 minutes or expand to 15. Written slide by slide: what goes ON the slide (few words) and what you SAY (the script). Read `01-MARKET-POSITION-AND-CRITIQUE.md` first — this pitch pre-empts its objections rather than hiding from them. Fill the `[brackets]` with your real numbers before ever pitching; pitching placeholder numbers is how you get remembered badly.

## The one-liner (memorize cold)

> "Stratos turns any startup idea into an investor-grade, fully-cited market research report in ten minutes, for $19 instead of $2,000."

## Slide-by-slide

### Slide 1 — Title
**On slide:** Stratos. "Analyst-grade market research, in minutes." Your name, contact.
**Say:** the one-liner, then: "Let me show you before I tell you." → 45-second live demo or the demo video: idea typed → clarifying questions → sections streaming with citations → PDF. The demo IS the pitch; everything after is supporting material.

### Slide 2 — Problem
**On slide:** 3 quotes (real ones from your beta tracker). "$2,000+ per report. 2–6 weeks. Or: ChatGPT with no sources."
**Say:** "Everyone making a business decision needs the same document — market size, competitors, trends, risks. Today you either pay analyst firms thousands and wait weeks, or paste into ChatGPT and get confident answers you can't verify or put in front of an investor. The document people actually need — structured, cited, current — has no product at the price point of the people who need it most."

### Slide 3 — Solution
**On slide:** the pipeline in 5 icons (clarify → research → verify → write → deliver). One screenshot of a report page with visible citations. "Every claim traced to a live source."
**Say:** "Stratos runs a nine-stage analyst workflow: it interviews you about the idea, plans a report structure for *that* idea, researches across search, news, academic and trend sources, and writes each section with enforced citations — a claim that can't be traced to evidence gets deleted, not published. Out comes a PDF you'd hand to your board." (If the W6-S2 audit is live: "Each report ships with a measured verified-citation rate — currently [X]%.")

### Slide 4 — Why now
**On slide:** three lines: LLM cost collapse. Deep Research proved demand. Trust is the gap.
**Say:** "Frontier labs just proved hundreds of millions of people want AI research — and simultaneously proved the gap: their output is a chat answer, not a decision document, and 'the model said so' doesn't survive a partner meeting. Inference costs fell far enough that our marginal cost per report is about [15] cents on a $19 price."

### Slide 5 — Market
**On slide:** $80B+ market research industry. Wedge: [X]M founders + consultants. Bottom-up: 1M target users × $230/yr ARPU.
**Say (honest version — investors smell inflated TAMs):** "We're not claiming the enterprise research market. Our wedge is the long tail it ignores: founders, consultants, and analysts who need research-shaped documents weekly but can't pay enterprise prices. Bottom-up, [your napkin math]. The expansion is where it gets big —" (→ slide 8).

### Slide 6 — Traction
**On slide:** ONLY real numbers: reports generated, WAU, completion rate, paying users, MRR, week-over-week growth. A growth curve if you have 6+ weeks of data.
**Say:** the numbers, then one retention story: "[Name], a fractional CMO, has generated [N] reports and told us she bills each into a $[X]k client engagement." If pre-revenue: show beta usage + the waitlist + 3 willingness-to-pay quotes, and say plainly "we monetize on [date]" — a date, not a vibe.

### Slide 7 — Business model & unit economics
**On slide:** Free 2/mo → $19 Starter → $49 Pro. Marginal cost ~$0.15/report. Gross margin >90%.
**Say:** "Self-serve SaaS. The margin math works at $19 because our pipeline budgets every search and model call. Churn is the known dragon for report tools — our answer is the monitoring tier: reports that re-research themselves monthly and alert on changes, converting a one-shot purchase into a subscription that's earning its keep." 

### Slide 8 — Moat & expansion (the slide skeptics came for)
**On slide:** Verification layer (audit scores). Vertical templates. Monitoring. Worker APIs.
**Say:** "Candidly: a pipeline alone is not a moat — anyone can chain model calls. What compounds for us: one, the verification layer — measured citation-accuracy per report type, tuned on every report we generate; two, per-vertical report templates that make output decision-ready in ways generic research isn't; three, monitoring turns documents into subscriptions; four, each pipeline stage — research, competitor intelligence, trend momentum, grounded writing — is independently exposable as an API. If model progress commoditizes generation, we're positioned in what doesn't commoditize: verification, structure, and workflow."

### Slide 9 — Competition
**On slide:** 2×2: axis 1 verifiability, axis 2 decision-document vs chat-answer. Deep Research products bottom-right, analyst firms top-left at 100× price, Stratos top-right.
**Say:** "Deep Research is the elephant — we position around it, not against it: they answer questions; we produce standardized, verifiable decision documents for a specific buyer and workflow. Analyst firms validate the price umbrella. The AI report-tool long tail competes with us on our axis — and mostly hasn't built verification."

### Slide 10 — Team
**On slide:** you, one line each: built the entire system solo end-to-end; shipped in [N] weeks.
**Say:** your genuine story + velocity evidence ("idea to paying users in [N] weeks, solo"). Velocity is the seed-stage team metric.

### Slide 11 — Ask
**On slide:** Raising $[X] pre-seed. 18 months. Milestones: $[X]k MRR, verification metrics published, monitoring tier live, [N] paying teams.
**Say:** "We're raising [X] to get to [milestone] — the proof points that make the Series A story about the market-intelligence platform, not the report generator."

## Q&A prep (the five you WILL get)

1. **"What if OpenAI adds report PDFs to Deep Research?"** → "They serve everyone, which means they template no one. Our buyer needs the TAM slide, the competitor quadrant, the format their investor expects. Verticalized document products have repeatedly co-existed with horizontal giants — and our verification metrics are a product decision they've shown no interest in making."
2. **"Is this just a wrapper?"** → Never defensive. "It's an orchestrated nine-stage pipeline — search, scraping, ranking, drafting, and a verification pass that deletes unsupported claims. The model is one component. Happy to walk through the architecture." (You have doc 01's classification memorized.)
3. **"Churn?"** → the slide-7 monitoring answer + real retention numbers or a named plan to get them.
4. **"Why you?"** → velocity + proximity to the user ("I am the user") + evidence you ship.
5. **"Moat, really?"** → slide 8, shorter: "Data from every generated report tunes verification and templates nobody else has. Early, yes. Compounding, also yes."

## Rules

- Demo before deck whenever the format allows. 
- Never argue with a skeptic's premise — agree with the risk, show the plan (doc 01 exists so you're never surprised).
- Send-ahead version: 8 slides max, no script, PDF. This doc is the *talk* track.
