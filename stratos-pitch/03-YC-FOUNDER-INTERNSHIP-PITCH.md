# 03 — Pitching a YC Founder for an Internship/Role

> Different game from the investor pitch: you're not selling the company, you're selling **yourself, with Stratos as the evidence**. A YC founder hiring early people cares about exactly three things: can you ship without supervision, do you understand users, and will you raise the team's velocity. Stratos proves all three — if presented right.

## 1. The positioning

You are not "a student who did a project". You are **someone who built and launched a production AI system solo** — orchestration, streaming, evidence pipelines, auth, payments, deployment — and can show the commit history, the live URL, and (best of all) usage numbers. Most applicants have coursework; you have a shipped system with users. Lead with that gap.

**The one-liner about yourself:**
> "I built and launched Stratos solo — an AI pipeline that turns a startup idea into a cited market-research PDF in ~10 minutes: nine Celery workers, event-driven orchestration, live-streaming UI, verified citations, deployed and used by [N] people. I want to bring that shipping speed to [their company]."

## 2. The cold email (the template)

Subject: `Built an AI research pipeline solo — want to do this for [Company]`

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm [you], and I built Stratos — type in a startup idea, get a fully-cited market research PDF in ~10 minutes. Live at [url]. One person, [N] weeks: FastAPI + Celery pipeline (9 workers: clarification interview, multi-source research, trend momentum, competitor discovery, grounded writing with citation verification), SSE-streamed Next.js frontend, deployed on Railway/Vercel with Google auth and Stripe. [N] reports generated by [N] users so far.
>
> I've been following [Company] since [specific, true thing — their launch post, a feature, a talk]. [One sentence connecting what you built to what they build — see §3.]
>
> I'd love to work with you as an intern/founding engineer. Two things I can show in a 15-minute call: the live product, and exactly how I'd apply the same pipeline discipline to [a specific problem of theirs].
>
> Live demo: [url] • Sample report: [pdf link] • Code walkthrough (5-min video): [loom]
>
> [You]

**Rules:** under 150 words before the links; one *specific* compliment (generic flattery = deleted); end with an offer to show, not a request for time. Send to the technical founder, not jobs@.

## 3. Mapping Stratos to THEIR problems (do this homework per company)

The email's magic sentence connects your system to their stack. Examples of the shape:

- Company does AI agents → "The hard parts of Stratos were exactly agent-infra problems: budgeted tool use, fail-soft provider fallbacks, validating LLM output before it ships. I'd love to work on your [agent runtime/evals]."
- Company does search/data → "I built the retrieval and credibility-ranking layer myself — provider abstraction across SerpAPI/Serper/Brave, SSRF-guarded scraping, evidence ranking. Directly relevant to [their product]."
- Company does document/workflow SaaS → "Stratos is a document-generation pipeline end to end — streaming drafts, citation integrity, PDF rendering. Your [feature] has the same skeleton."
- Company does devtools → "I used [their tool or its competitor] building Stratos; here are three things I hit that your roadmap could fix."

If you can't write this sentence for a company, don't email that company yet.

## 4. The 15-minute call, scripted

1. **Min 0–4 — live demo**, on the production URL (never localhost). Type THEIR company's space as the idea ("AI-powered [their category] tools") — the report is about a market they know, so they can judge quality instantly. This move alone gets you remembered.
2. **Min 4–8 — architecture walk-through** (one prepared diagram: the pipeline from doc `../stratos-launch-plan/02-TARGET-ARCHITECTURE.md`). Narrate decisions, not features: "I gated section-writing on both research and trend completion with a timeout so a dead feed can't hang the pipeline" — decisions show engineering judgment, which is what they're hiring.
3. **Min 8–12 — honest limitations** (from `01-MARKET-POSITION-AND-CRITIQUE.md`): "It's an orchestrated pipeline, not an autonomous agent — deliberately, because verifiability is the product. The unbuilt hard parts are hallucination auditing and semantic retrieval; here's my plan for both." Volunteering critique is the single strongest senior-signal a junior can send.
4. **Min 12–15 — the ask + the plan**: "First two weeks at [Company], I'd want to [specific contribution from §3 homework]. What would be most useful for me to prototype before a trial week?" — offer a work-trial project; YC founders love paying for proof over promises.

## 5. Supporting assets checklist

- [ ] Live production URL that works every time (run the smoke test before every call — a dead demo undoes everything)
- [ ] One 5-minute Loom: 2 min demo + 3 min code/architecture tour
- [ ] A public sample-report PDF relevant to their industry
- [ ] GitHub with a real README (pin the repo); commit history tells your velocity story for you
- [ ] The one-page architecture diagram, exported as an image
- [ ] Your X/LinkedIn build-in-public thread (doc `04-X-CONTENT-PLAN.md` output doubles as your portfolio)

## 6. What NOT to do

- Don't pitch Stratos-the-startup to someone you're asking for a job — pick one. Asking for internship = Stratos is your *proof of work*, and be gracious if they ask "why not work on your own thing?" ("I want reps inside a great team; Stratos taught me how much I don't know about [scaling/GTM/etc.].")
- Don't oversell ("agentic AGI system") — you'll be interviewed by people who read code. Doc 01's honest classification is your armor.
- Don't send the same email to 50 companies. Ten companies with real §3 homework beats fifty spray-and-pray, every time.
